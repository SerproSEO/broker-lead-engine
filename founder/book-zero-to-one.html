Zero to One

Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future

Why This Book Changed Everything

This book taught me to stop competing and start creating monopolies. Instead of fighting for scraps in crowded markets, I now build businesses that dominate tiny niches.

Key Concepts That Matter

1. Competition is for Losers

Competitive markets destroy profits. Build monopolies by being so good at something specific that no one can compete.

How I Apply This:

Instead of "marketing agency," I built "insurance broker lead generation systems." Specific monopoly beats general competition.

2. The Power Law

One market/product/distribution channel will produce outsized returns. Everything else is noise. Find your power law and focus everything there.

How I Apply This:

Discovered insurance brokers were my power law market. Dropped everything else. Revenue increased 500%.

3. Start Small, Monopolize, Then Expand

Dominate a small market completely before expanding. Amazon started with just books. Facebook started with just Harvard.

How I Apply This:

Started with cyber insurance brokers in Massachusetts. Dominated. Then expanded to other insurance types and states.

4. Secrets: What Important Truth Do Few People Agree With You On?

Great businesses are built on secrets—things you believe that others don't. These contrarian truths create monopoly opportunities.

How I Apply This:

My secret: AI + automation can replace 80% of marketing agency work. Built business on this before others believed it.

Quotes That Stick

"Competition is for losers."

"All failed companies are the same: they failed to escape competition."

"Brilliant thinking is rare, but courage is in even shorter supply."

"The most contrarian thing of all is not to oppose the crowd but to think for yourself."

My Top 10 Takeaways

  • Build monopolies, not competitive businesses
  • 10x improvement is easier to sell than 10% improvement
  • Start with a tiny market you can dominate
  • Distribution is as important as product
  • Sales matters—even great products don't sell themselves
  • First mover advantage is a myth—last mover advantage is real
  • Definite optimism beats indefinite optimism
  • Secrets exist—look where others aren't looking
  • Focus on one thing and do it better than anyone
  • The best businesses are boring monopolies printing money
