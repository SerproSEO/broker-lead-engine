Influence

The Psychology of Persuasion

The Science Behind Every Yes

After infiltrating sales organizations, cults, and con artists for three years, Cialdini discovered six universal principles that drive human compliance. These aren't tricks—they're hardwired psychological triggers that work because we're human. Master these and you'll never struggle to persuade again.

The Six Weapons of Influence

🎁

1. Reciprocity: The Power of Going First

People feel obligated to return favors. Give first, receive second. The key: give something personalized, unexpected, and meaningful.

The Hare Krishna flower strategy: Force a gift, then ask for a donation. Success rate: 500% increase.

How I Apply This:

I give value before I ever pitch. Free audits, industry reports, competitive analysis. By the time I ask for the sale, they feel they owe me. Close rate increased 35%.

🔒

2. Commitment & Consistency: Start Small, Go Big

People align their actions with their commitments, especially public ones. Get a small yes, then ladder up to bigger asks.

The foot-in-the-door technique: Small request → compliance → bigger request → higher compliance.

How I Apply This:

First ask: "Can I send you our industry report?" (95% yes). Second: "Can we discuss it?" (70% yes). Third: "Can we pilot our solution?" (45% yes). Each yes makes the next more likely.

👥

3. Social Proof: Follow the Crowd

People look to others' behavior to guide their own, especially when uncertain. Show them that people like them are already doing it.

Hotel towel reuse: "Most guests reuse" = 26% increase. "Most guests in this room reuse" = 33% increase.

How I Apply This:

"73% of insurance brokers your size use our system" beats any feature list. I name drop similar clients, share case studies, show logos. FOMO is real and powerful.

❤️

4. Liking: We Say Yes to People We Like

People buy from those they like. Factors: similarity, compliments, cooperation, attractiveness, familiarity.

Tupperware party genius: Friends selling to friends. Conversion rate: 700% higher than cold sales.

How I Apply This:

I find genuine commonalities: hometown, hobbies, challenges. I compliment specifically: "Your LinkedIn post on digital transformation was brilliant." Connection before pitch, always.

🎓

5. Authority: Trust the Expert

People defer to authority figures and experts. Titles, clothes, and trappings all trigger automatic compliance.

Lab coat effect: Same person, add a lab coat = 300% increase in compliance with medical advice.

How I Apply This:

I lead with credentials subtly: "In my 10 years specializing in insurance tech..." I display certifications, quote industry reports, reference big-name clients. Expertise sells.

⏰

6. Scarcity: Now or Never

People value things more when they're less available. Loss aversion is twice as powerful as gain seeking.

Cookie jar study: 2 cookies taste better than 10. Cookies that go from 10 to 2? Even better.

How I Apply This:

"We only onboard 3 new clients per quarter" (true). "This pricing ends Friday" (also true). "Two competitors are evaluating this" (when true). Authentic scarcity beats fake urgency.

The Unity Principle (7th Principle - Added Later)

We say yes to those we consider "one of us." Shared identity trumps similarity. Family, ethnicity, politics, causes—these create instant bonds.

How I Apply This:

"As a fellow entrepreneur who bootstrapped from nothing..." or "We both know what it's like building in this economy..." Shared struggle creates shared identity.

Principles That Changed How I Sell

"The rule for reciprocation...is so strong that it can overcome dislike for the person making the request."

"Consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds...except when it comes to human psychology, where it rules."

"We view a behavior as correct in a given situation to the degree that we see others performing it."

"The way to love anything is to realize that it may be lost."

My Top 10 Takeaways

  • Give first, ask second - reciprocity creates obligation
  • Get small commitments before big ones - consistency drives compliance
  • Show others doing it - social proof beats logical proof
  • Find genuine commonalities - we buy from people we like
  • Establish expertise early - authority triggers automatic compliance
  • Make it scarce (authentically) - loss aversion is powerful
  • Stack multiple principles - they compound exponentially
  • These work because they're shortcuts our brains use to decide
  • Ethical influence helps people make good decisions faster
  • Defense: Recognize when these are being used on you
