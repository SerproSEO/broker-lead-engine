Understanding E&O Insurance Fundamentals

Errors and omissions (E&O) insurance, also known as professional liability insurance, protects businesses and professionals against claims of inadequate work, negligent acts, errors, or omissions in professional services. This essential coverage forms the backbone of risk management for service-based businesses across all industries.

What E&O Insurance Covers

Professional Negligence

Claims alleging failure to exercise reasonable care in providing professional services

Errors and Mistakes

Unintentional errors in professional work that result in client financial losses

Omissions

Failure to perform required professional duties or meet contractual obligations

Defense Costs

Legal fees, court costs, and settlement expenses for covered claims

Key E&O Insurance Terms

  • Claims-Made Policy: Covers claims reported during the policy period, regardless of when the incident occurred
  • Occurrence Policy: Covers incidents that happen during the policy period, regardless of when claims are filed
  • Retroactive Date: The earliest date from which the policy will cover past incidents
  • Extended Reporting Period: Coverage for claims reported after policy expiration for incidents during the policy period
  • Aggregate Limit: Maximum amount the insurer will pay for all claims during the policy period

Types of E&O Coverage Options

Primary E&O Coverage

Primary E&O insurance provides first-layer protection against professional liability claims. This foundational coverage typically includes:

Basic E&O Coverage

  • Professional negligence claims
  • Defense cost coverage
  • Settlement payments
  • Court-ordered judgments

Starting at $1,200-$3,000 annually

Enhanced E&O Coverage

  • All basic coverage features
  • Prior acts coverage
  • Extended reporting periods
  • Regulatory proceeding coverage
  • Crisis management services

Typically $3,000-$8,000 annually

Comprehensive E&O Coverage

  • All enhanced features
  • Cyber liability integration
  • Employment practices coverage
  • Third-party coverage
  • International coverage

$8,000-$25,000+ annually

Excess and Umbrella E&O Coverage

For businesses requiring higher limits, excess and umbrella policies provide additional layers of protection:

Excess E&O Insurance

Provides additional coverage above primary policy limits for the same types of claims. Follow-form coverage that mirrors the primary policy terms.

Umbrella E&O Insurance

Broader coverage that may fill gaps in primary policies while providing higher limits. Can cover exposures not included in the primary policy.

Policy Limits and Deductibles Guide

Selecting Appropriate Policy Limits

Choosing the right policy limits requires careful analysis of potential exposure and financial capacity. Consider these factors:

Exposure Assessment Factors

  • Client Size: Larger clients create higher potential damages
  • Service Complexity: Complex services increase error probability
  • Contract Values: Higher value projects = higher potential losses
  • Industry Standards: What competitors and clients expect
  • Personal Assets: Assets at risk in excess of policy limits

Common Policy Limit Structures

$1M per claim / $1M aggregate

Suitable for: Small professional practices, low-risk services

Industries: Consultants, small tech firms, certain healthcare practices

$2M per claim / $2M aggregate

Suitable for: Mid-sized firms, moderate risk services

Industries: Mid-market consulting, established practices

$5M per claim / $5M aggregate

Suitable for: Larger firms, higher risk services

Industries: Large consulting firms, complex technology services

$10M+ per claim / $10M+ aggregate

Suitable for: Large organizations, high-risk industries

Industries: Major law firms, large healthcare systems, investment advisors

Aggregate vs. Per Claim Limits

Understanding Limit Structures

Per Claim Limit: Maximum the insurer will pay for any single claim or related claims

Aggregate Limit: Maximum the insurer will pay for all claims during the policy period

Aggregate Reinstatement Options

  • Standard Aggregate: Depletes with each claim payment
  • Reinstatement Provision: Allows aggregate restoration for additional premium
  • Defense Cost Arrangements: Inside vs. outside the limits

Contract Terms Negotiation

Critical Policy Provisions

Consent to Settle Clauses

Carrier Options:

  • Full Consent: Insurer cannot settle without your approval
  • Qualified Consent: You can refuse settlement but bear excess costs
  • No Consent: Insurer has full settlement authority

Recommendation: Negotiate for qualified consent to maintain some control while limiting financial exposure.

Definition of Professional Services

Ensure the policy definition captures all services your business provides:

  • Current Services: All services currently offered
  • Planned Services: Services you plan to offer
  • Incidental Services: Ancillary or support services
  • Broad Definition: Avoid overly restrictive language
Geographic Coverage

Verify coverage territory matches your business operations:

  • Worldwide Coverage: For international operations
  • US/Canada Coverage: Standard for North American businesses
  • Jurisdiction Clauses: Where legal proceedings must occur
  • Choice of Law: Which state's laws govern the policy
